# Maison Passage Gateway Pair Explorer Design Notes

## 1. Product Overview

`Maison Passage Gateway Pair Explorer` is a Next.js app for overseas travel planning.
It helps a traveler choose one departure country, compare multiple destination countries,
expand the search to nearby gateway airports when useful, and identify the cheapest
pair of one-way tickets before handing off to live search such as Skyscanner.

The product remains intentionally focused on the early decision of how to enter and leave a region.
It does not try to optimize the internal route between the arrival airport and the return
departure airport in the MVP.

## 3. Current Product Behavior And Next Design Direction

### 3.1 Current behavior we are preserving

- An overseas-travel gateway-pair comparison app.
- A flow that compares the cheapest outbound one-way and cheapest return one-way separately.
- Honest estimated pricing that hands off to live search for final confirmation.
- Fare ranges, confidence labels, and estimate-basis notes that make deterministic pricing clearly provisional.
- Flexible date exploration for travelers who only know a target season such as July through September.
- Approximate stay-length ranges, such as 26 to 36 days, before the traveler commits to exact dates.
- Exact-date mode for travelers who already know the outbound day.

### 3.2 What is out of scope

- Booking, payment, or ticket management.
- Automatic internal routing after arrival in the destination region.
- User accounts or collaborative editing.
- Hotels, visas, exchange rates, and full itinerary planning.

### 3.3 Approved roadmap

- `Sprint 5`: Grand Tour Ledger Redesign
- `Sprint 6`: Gateway Pair Ticketing
- `Sprint 7`: Live Fare Enrichment

Sprint 6 should:

- Keep the Grand Tour Ledger premium dossier shell intact.
- Reframe the planner and result UI around two one-way tickets rather than open-jaw wording.
- Expand the destination search pool to nearby gateway airports for Europe.
- Keep flexible month comparison and stay-window logic intact.
- Keep fare ranges, confidence labels, airport coverage, alternatives, and Skyscanner handoff intact.

## 4. UX And Design Direction

### 4.1 Tone

- Archival, cinematic, worldly, and assured.
- Brand-led rather than dashboard-led.
- Bolder and more distinctive than the prior generic luxury editorial shell.
- Trustworthy and deliberate rather than crowded or hyper-animated.

### 4.2 Palette And Materials

- Parchment and warm paper tones as the main surfaces.
- Oxblood, espresso, brass, and near-black for accents and structure.
- Optional atlas green or stamp red only in restrained support roles.
- Avoid bright utility blues and avoid generic beige-only luxury minimalism.

### 4.3 Layout principles

- The page should read like a premium travel dossier assembled for a Grand Tour.
- Each major section should feel like a spread, insert, or annotated rail.
- The planner atelier and result folio should feel like the operational heart of the experience.
- Evidence cues such as proof strips, annotations, and dividers should build trust without turning the page into a dashboard.

### 4.4 Required section order

- Header
- Cover spread
- Proof strip
- Planner atelier
- Result folio
- Story/method rail
- Archetypes
- Concierge CTA
- Footer

### 4.5 Section intent

- The header should start refined and light, then gain a subtle readable archival surface on scroll.
- The cover spread should fill the first viewport and feel like a bold dossier cover rather than a generic hero block.
- The proof strip should sit immediately below the cover spread and communicate trust cues such as estimate honesty, airport coverage scale, or method notes.
- The planner atelier should contain the full overseas gateway-pair planning form, including exact-date and flexible-window controls.
- The result folio should frame the recommended two one-way tickets, gateway summary, supporting alternatives, and uncertainty cues as a premium results spread.
- The story/method rail should explain both the gateway-first two-ticket logic and the flexible month plus stay-window method.
- The archetypes section should present at least three refined traveler profiles or planning styles.
- The concierge CTA should feel bespoke and restrained.
- The footer should close the dossier with a dark premium tone.

## 5. Functional Requirements

### 5.1 Planner atelier inputs

- Departure country selector.
- Date search mode selector:
  - exact date mode for a known outbound date and fixed stay length.
  - flexible month mode for selecting multiple target outbound months.
- Flexible stay-length range selector with minimum and maximum days.
- Passenger count selector.
- Cabin class selector.
- Multi-select destination country picker.
- Direct-first toggle.
- Compare action.

### 5.2 Search behavior

- Selected destination countries represent the traveler’s headline stay countries.
- When a selected destination country is in Europe, the search pool may widen to nearby European gateway airports.
- The planner searches the cheapest outbound one-way first.
- After the cheapest outbound date is selected, the planner searches return dates from that date plus the selected stay-length range.
- The planner presents the result as two one-way tickets and a combined estimate.

### 5.3 Result folio behavior

- Cheapest outbound one-way ticket.
- Cheapest return one-way ticket.
- Combined estimated total.
- Per-person estimate.
- Fare ranges and confidence labels.
- Estimate basis for recommended legs.
- Clear explanation that a gateway airport outside the selected stay country may win.
- Clear explanation that internal travel is excluded.

### 5.4 Alternatives and handoff

- Alternative outbound entry options.
- Alternative return exit options.
- Per-country airport coverage details.
- Primary Skyscanner outbound and return one-way handoff links.
- Optional combined reference handoff link.
- Honest language that live search is the final fare source of truth.

## 6. Acceptance Focus

- The first impression should feel like a Grand Tour Ledger dossier, not a generic premium landing page.
- The page should follow the required section order from header through footer.
- The fixed header should remain readable after scroll.
- The first viewport should be a true cover spread.
- The proof strip should appear immediately after the cover spread.
- The planner atelier and result folio should be visually prominent and clearly distinct.
- The story/method rail and archetypes section should both be present.
- The compare action should stay disabled or blocked until the required trip inputs are set.
- A sample journey such as Japan departure plus France, United Kingdom, and Germany should produce a cheapest outbound one-way, cheapest return one-way, and combined estimate.
- A Germany-led search may still surface a gateway such as Milan when it is cheaper than flying directly into Germany.
- A flexible sample using July, August, and September plus a 26 to 36 day stay range should produce a concrete outbound date and a concrete return date.
- The result should explain the compared outbound month window, derived return window, stay range, gateway pool, and compared date counts.
- The estimate should show ranges and confidence labels so users do not mistake reference prices for final live inventory.
- The result should explain the two-ticket assumption and the excluded internal travel.
- The page should work at roughly 390px mobile width and 1280px desktop width without obvious horizontal overflow.

## 7. Demo Flow

1. Open the app and confirm that it feels like a Grand Tour Ledger dossier rather than a generic premium travel page or dense utility dashboard.
2. Review the fixed header and scroll to confirm the readable archival surface treatment.
3. Review the cover spread and confirm that the first viewport feels bold, cinematic, and dossier-like.
4. Confirm that the proof strip appears directly after the cover spread and communicates trust cues.
5. In the planner atelier, select Japan as the departure country and France, United Kingdom, and Germany as destination countries.
6. Keep flexible mode selected, choose July, August, and September, set the stay range to 26 to 36 days, then run the comparison.
7. Confirm the result folio shows the cheapest outbound one-way date and the return one-way chosen from the derived return window.
8. Confirm the compared outbound and return date counts, stay range, fare ranges, confidence labels, gateway summary, and estimate basis are visible.
9. Review the story/method rail and confirm that the two-ticket gateway concept and flexible travel-window logic are explained clearly.
10. Review the archetypes section and confirm at least three refined profiles or planning styles are present.
11. Confirm the internal-travel exclusion note and the one-way Skyscanner handoff links.

## 8. Harness Notes

### 8.1 Verified entrypoint

The verified Codex harness entrypoint in this workspace is `agents/orchestrator_codex.py`.
Available commands are:

- `python agents/orchestrator_codex.py plan "<description>"`
- `python agents/orchestrator_codex.py generate <sprint>`
- `python agents/orchestrator_codex.py evaluate <sprint> http://localhost:3000`
- `python agents/orchestrator_codex.py autodev "<description>" --sprint N --max-iterations M --url http://localhost:3000`
- `python agents/orchestrator_codex.py status`

### 8.2 Verified autodev behavior

- `plan` updates `specs/spec.json`.
- Each iteration runs `generate`.
- If `build/package.json` exists, the harness attempts `npm install` in `build/`.
- The harness then starts `npm run dev` and waits for `http://localhost:3000`.
- The harness runs `evaluate`.
- A `FAIL` report is passed into the next `generate` attempt as evaluator feedback.
- A `PASS` report ends the loop.

### 8.3 Dev server note

`build/package.json` maps `npm run dev` to `build/scripts/dev.ps1`.
That script attempts to free port `3000` on Windows before starting Next.js.
If the port cannot be freed, it exits with a clear error.
